Latencies are [measured, bench/lm18/choose.py and bench.py -> choose.json, results.jsonl]. Ratios and differences are [derived, those scripts -> decision-128.json]. The two sessions are not a cross-session speedup comparison. The selected compiler contract is [measured, kernels/megakernel_mt2/build.sh -> build-selected-m*.log].

## Gate decision and status

**Killed on performance, complete on diagnosis and correctness.** The initial preload selection was already above the binding 1.5x limit. No further performance optimization or context-2048 timing was attempted. A separate numerical compiler-contract fix was needed to satisfy sequential equality. The final selected kernel passes the stricter sequential-exact gate for both modes at every M from 1 through 5, repeats bitwise, and has zero production spills. Its final batch M4/M1 ratio is 2.185353x, so it is not a replacement for the LM-14 engine [measured/derived, check_gate.py, bench.py].

Owned files are `kernels/megakernel_mt2/`, `bench/lm18/`, and this report. Existing LM-14, v2, scale and validator packages were not edited. No git writes or private-research copies were made.

## Diagnosis

The added cost is **serial column-local operand, arithmetic and reduction work**, not an increase in weight bytes or an LDS bank conflict. Preloading activation operands removes a confirmed GEMM load-to-use/scoreboard component, but does not remove the rest of the column work. After that fix, the largest remaining full-pass increment is actually outside GEMM: attention and the serial prologues together account for about 61% of the diagnostic M4-minus-M1 phase increment [derived, profile.py, analyze.py -> profile.json, attribution.json].

This corrects the scope of the original hypothesis. The throughput bound counts LDS and HFMA2 capacity; it does not account for all load-to-use dependencies, column reductions, barrier/control work, or the serial non-GEMM column loops. Removing `volatile` alone did not fix the problem. The compiler was already scheduling groups of loads ahead of their consumers.

### SASS, dependencies and bank pattern

Static NC=1 loops represent gate_up/qkv/lm_head. NC=3 represents down. Counts include row arithmetic, output/reduction paths and loop control. They are not retired-instruction or stall counters [derived, sass.py -> sass.json and sass-*.txt].

| loop | thread instructions | instructions/weight | LDS | HFMA2 | encoded wait-mask sites | HFMA2 wait-mask sites |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| original M1, NC=1 | 154 | 4.8125 | 16 | 32 | 19 | 11 |
| original M4, NC=1 | 328 | 10.2500 | 64 | 80 | 61 | 34 |
| preload M1, NC=1 | 135 | 4.2188 | 0 | 32 | 12 | 2 |
| preload M4, NC=1 | 261 | 8.1563 | 0 | 80 | 30 | 1 |
| original M1, NC=3 | 408 | 4.2500 | 48 | 96 | 49 | 36 |
| original M4, NC=3 | 818 | 8.5208 | 192 | 240 | 168 | 137 |
| selected fallback M4, NC=3 | 815 | 8.4896 | 192 | 240 | 74 | 45 |

NC=1 has 32 weights per thread, NC=3 has 96. The INT4 chunk loads and weight bytes do not multiply with M. Per chunk, the 16 dequant HFMA2 instructions are followed by 16 dot-product HFMA2 instructions per column. Thus M4 has 80 HFMA2 instructions per chunk versus 32 at M1. Each column retains four independent FP16 chains, four updates per chain; all columns retain the same local and cross-lane reduction order. Per-column static instruction density for original NC=1 is 10.25/4 = 2.5625 instructions per weight-column at M4, versus 4.8125 at M1 [derived, sass.py and gemm_original.cuh].

The scalar address is `words[pair*cpr + lane + 32*t]`. Every supported `cpr` is a multiple of 32, so the bank index is the lane index. Consecutive lanes access distinct banks. This is an address-level bank-pattern proof, not a hardware bank-conflict counter [derived, gemm_original.cuh, mega_mt.cu]. The original emitted LDS instructions are scalar `LDS.U`, not a vector load that supplies all columns at once.

Original M4 SASS has many load read/write-barrier allocations and dependent HFMA2 wait masks. Preloading NC=1 operands moves those LDS instructions outside the row loop, and removes nearly all HFMA2 wait-mask sites inside that loop. NC=3 still carries both loads and many dependent consumers. The table decodes the Turing instruction control word using the assumed ISA bit layout documented in `sass.py`; a wait-mask site is a possible scoreboard dependency, not a measured number of waiting cycles [assumed ISA layout; derived static counts, sass.py]. No Nsight stall-counter claim is made.

### Clock64 evidence per shape

`shape_probe.cu` instruments every sampled warp with operand-dependent clock boundaries. The initial fine-grained probe splits weight load, dequant, activation load, FMA, local reduction and warp reduction. It showed activation/FMA time growing with M while weight load time stayed roughly constant. Initial 1024-thread diagnostic M3/M4 builds spilled; the diagnostic was narrowed to 512 threads before the retained spill-free comparison. No spilled diagnostic is used as a production timing [measured, build-shape-*.log, results.jsonl].

The coarse selected-loop probe uses 128 diagnostic threads to retain timers without spills. It groups operands to expose serialized latency, rather than pretending to count production stalls. The first phase profile only had one `other` bucket. A separate, spill-free detailed clock build was therefore necessary; the aggregate alone cannot identify its subcomponents. This is additional profiling, not another optimization. RMSNorm and residual math share `xresidual`; q/k norm, RoPE and KV materialization are fused and overlap in the attention prologue. Their times cannot honestly be separated into additional non-overlapping rows with these boundaries. At M1, K append is inside attention, so its separate append row is zero, not an absence of KV writes. The sampler here is the inherited argmax token selection, not stochastic sampling.

The detailed other-phase increments excluding grid waits total about 0.7713 ms, versus 0.7512 ms in the coarse build. Detailed attention increments total about 0.5857 ms, versus 0.5745 ms in the coarse build. These are close diagnostic decompositions, not the same trace replay; extra boundaries and clocks alter timing. There is no parallelization across M in batch attention: `mega_mt` calls `attn2` once per sequence, with a CTA synchronization between calls [derived, detail_profile.py; source, mega_mt.cu].

## Fixes tried

- **Nonvolatile control:** remove the volatile scalar activation load. Gate_up M4 0.0251685 ms versus original 0.0251470 ms in that paired shape session. Full M4 4.331520 ms versus rebuilt-original 4.332416 ms in candidate selection. No meaningful gain [measured, shape_probe.py, choose.py].
- **Interleaved accumulator chains:** decode one pair and reuse it across columns before advancing. Gate_up M4 0.0252160 ms and down M4 0.0175525 ms versus original 0.0251470/0.0178595 ms in the same shape session. It did not solve scaling. The full M4 build introduced 8 bytes of spills and was rejected before production timing [measured, shape_probe.py, build-interleaved-m4.log].
- **Register activation preload:** move NC=1 activation LDS outside the output-row loop, retain four chains per column, and use 512 threads at M>1 to stay spill-free. Paired M4 full-pass saving 0.574112 ms and GEMM-only saving 0.632576 ms. Wider NC=2/3 retains the nonvolatile fallback to avoid an oversized register footprint [measured/derived, choose.py].
- **Numerical contract:** disable compiler FP32 multiply-add contraction for the final selected build. This fixes cross-M numerical drift without changing the explicit FP16 `__hfma2` GEMM arithmetic [measured, check_contraction.py, check_gate.py].
- **MMA fragments:** not attempted in LM-18. LM-14 already has a slower fragment prototype, but that is not a timing result for a new LM-18 MMA design. A different tiled fragment mapping remains an open thread, not a fabricated experiment.

## Correctness and compiler-contract cost

The first preload gate was repeatable but not sequential-exact at M3-5. Max drift was 0.0462799 while HF max error remained 0.1302333. The preserved `gate-contracted.json` records that failure. Isolated gate_up/down outputs at M2-4 are bitwise equal to M1 on the numerical shape check, which rules out a simple change to their INT4 dot-product arithmetic on that input [measured, check_gate.py, check_shapes.py -> shape-correctness.json].

The focused contraction check confirmed the hypothesis: the contracted M4 prompt-2 difference was 0.0445862; rebuilding with `--fmad=false` made it exactly zero. The exact FP32 phase/site was not separately bisected; the compiler-contraction cause is confirmed, not a claim that one specific norm instruction has been identified [measured, check_contraction.py -> contraction.json].

Final `check_gate.py` uses sequential tolerance **0.0**, stricter than LM-14's 0.1. Both modes at every M=1..5 pass across all three reference prompts and 64 teacher-forced positions. Maximum HF error is 0.1874557 versus the v2 baseline's 0.2114177; sequential difference is 0.0, repeat is bitwise, and there are zero nonfinite logits, hard flips or listed near ties [measured, check_gate.py -> gate.json]. The final kernel is not claimed bitwise equal to LM-14 or numerically closer to HF than the contracted preload candidate.

## Next fix target and honest limits

The largest individual non-GEMM increment is `pro_attnc`, +0.363885 ms, and batch attention as a whole still runs sequences serially. The most bounded next fix is **process `pro_attnc` columns in pairs using both 256-thread groups of the 512-thread CTA**. Currently each call uses only 256 threads and ends in a CTA barrier, then the next column repeats the work. Perfect two-way overlap would reduce the measured M4 combine/staging phase from 0.492094 to about 0.246047 ms, saving about **0.246 ms** [derived, analyze.py -> attribution.json; assumed perfect parallel efficiency]. That is a testable expectation, not a measured speedup. It would not meet the 1.5x full-pass limit by itself.

- The removable NC=1 LDS component is confirmed, but the original ~0.85 ms/token excess is not uniquely attributable to one hardware stall counter. The report separates real phase measurements from the explicitly assumed finer allocation.
- The first preload variant failed strict sequential equality. The final compiler-contract build fixes it and passes the complete gate; it still fails the performance kill.
- Batch attention, residual/norm, attention-output combine, SiLU and argmax retain serial column loops. Sharing weights does not parallelize those phases.
- No context-2048 timing, speculative-decoding break-even claim, scale port, new MMA fragment design, or race-hardening result is claimed. The binding kill stopped those extensions.
- This is the requested LM-14-equivalent runtime gate plus strict sequential equality, not a substitute for LM-17's hardened gate tiers.
