## Matvec module (acceptance from integrator)

`k_gemv<NC>` is now a verbatim `gemv.cu` `gemv_kernel<I4,NC,false>` port:
by-value `Desc` in kernel param space, `Chunk`/`load_chunk`/`dot_chunk` with
`__ldg` + 4 half2 accumulators + 0x6400 magic, x staged in SMEM then held per
lane in registers, RPW=2, `__launch_bounds__(256, 4|2)`, grid
`min(ceil(out/16), occ*36)`.

- ptxas: `k_gemv<1>` = **62 regs, 0 spills** (wave-1: 62 regs, 0 spills);
  `k_gemv<2>` 95 regs, `k_gemv<3>` 122 regs — all 0 local ops. [measured]
- `mega_kernel` (persistent, 36 CTAs, `__launch_bounds__(256,1)`):
  **214 regs, 0 spills**. [measured]
- lm_head as a separate kernel inside this engine: **0.233 ms** [measured,
  `mk_time_gemv1`, at 1005 MHz] — ≥ wave-1's 0.22 ms at boost clock.
- Full 113-GEMV sequence: 1.52 ms at 1005 MHz [measured, `mk_time_gemvs`];
  wave-1's graph-captured reference is 1.306 ms (242.5 GB/s) at boost.

**Root cause of the earlier "regression":** a stale `#define RPW 12` from a
chunked-era sweep silently overrode `RPW=2`, blowing the register budget
(255→spills → 100 STL + 119 LDL in SASS). Not a structural difference; the
report of local-memory traffic was the spill diagnostic doing its job.

## Megakernel design

- 36 persistent CTAs × 256 threads (1/SM, unbounded launch → 214 regs,
  **0 spills**). 72 CTAs (2/SM, 128-reg cap) reintroduces 228 B of spills and
  is slower at every ctx (2.79/6.79 ms at 128/8192). [measured]
- One launch executes N decode steps: flags slab = steps × 286 counters;
  cross-step dependency via fid −1 = previous step's `argc_done`.
- Schedule IR v1 JSONs regenerated for nb=36:
  `schedules/mk_ctx{128,2048,8192}.json` (5376/6468/6496 tasks, 286 flags,
  14 buffers) — `validator/target/release/schedcheck`: **ACCEPT** all three.
- Task shapes: contiguous per-block GEMV row ranges (warps stride WARPS
  inside the range), attn position slices (nslice = 36/72/72; sweep at ctx
  8192: nslice 36→8.68 ms, 72→9.12, 144→9.90 [measured] — fewest slices is
  best but still below baseline).

## Honest negatives

1. **The persistent kernel is slower than a CUDA graph of separate kernels
   at every context.** At ctx 128 the flag-spin chain (~170 serialized
   producer→consumer edges, each an L2 acquire/release round trip +
   2×`__syncthreads`) costs slightly more than graph-node dispatch
   (0.61 µs/node × ~285). The gap grows with context: at 8192, attention
   slices spread over 36 CTAs starve bandwidth versus the graph's 72-block
   attention kernels (memory-bound op wants 16 warps/SM; the spill-free
   matvec wants 1 CTA/SM — an irreconcilable occupancy split inside one
   kernel image).
2. Non-interleaved timing can mask a >2× clock swing; all reported ratios
   come from interleaved runs.
