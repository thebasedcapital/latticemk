# One decode grid on a Turing GPU: gains and failed bets

## Abstract

On a Quadro RTX 4000, a persistent CUDA megakernel with GPTQ INT4-g128 weights runs batch-1 greedy decode for Qwen3-0.6B at 578.6, 416.6 and 221.6 tokens/s at context depths 128, 2048 and 8192. Same-session, interleaved llama.cpp Q4_0 with flash attention and fp16 KV reaches 352.7, 284.3 and 187.6 tokens/s. The paired speed ratios are 1.640, 1.465 and 1.181. On Qwen3-1.7B the ratios fall to 1.266, 1.217 and 1.094. GPTQ has lower WikiText-2 perplexity than Q4_0 on both models, but Q4_K_M has lower perplexity than GPTQ; its timings are not quality-matched comparisons. The main gain is removing small-kernel dispatch and phase handoffs without starving memory-level parallelism. Several plausible ways to go faster did not work. Those failures explain the scope of the result better than the peak ratio does.

## Machine and engine

The card is a Quadro RTX 4000, Turing sm_75, with 8 GB nominal memory and 36 SMs. A device read microbenchmark measured 406.7 GB/s, against a 416.1 GB/s specification. The SM clock could not be locked without root access. This is the 2018 card, not an Ada or Hopper evaluation. We use the measured read ceiling only for a bytes-per-step bandwidth proxy: packed weight bytes plus full fp16 KV reads divided by elapsed time. Neither this proxy nor its percentage of roofline is a hardware DRAM-counter measurement.

The engine keeps one grid resident for a decode step. The timed kernel launches 36 CTAs of 1024 threads, uses 64 registers per thread, and has no ptxas spills. Each CTA stays resident while the grid proceeds through projection, normalization, RoPE, attention, MLP and argmax phases. Grid-wide counters synchronize phase boundaries. The activation and residual data needed by fused prologues live in shared memory. The Qwen3-0.6B kernel accumulates some dot-product partials in fp16; the scaled Qwen3-1.7B kernel accumulates those products in fp32 after the fp16 path failed its logit gate. Both use GPTQ-packed INT4 weights with group size 128 and fp16 KV. Weight quantization is not the novelty here. In the 0.6B kernel, swapping RTN for GPTQ left the packed format and kernel unchanged; the interleaved ctx-128 speed ratio was 0.9969.

A separate Rust checker under `validator/` validates the static schedule IR before a kernel is admitted. Its fuzz campaign reported zero false accepts and zero false rejects over 10,000 generated schedules; the shipped context-specific schedules also pass. That is a schedule safety check, not a proof of floating-point equivalence or of the CUDA implementation. One synchronization fork later passed schedule checking while its actual counter implementation had a race; only runtime checks caught it.

## Measurement and correctness

The decode comparison is batch-1, warm-cache, greedy per-token latency at fixed context depth. Qwen3-0.6B's matched pair alternates v2 CUDA-event slices and llama-bench Q4_0 subprocesses within one harness, using 27 v2 and 30 llama timed samples per context. Qwen3-1.7B uses the same counts and alternating scheme. The v2 timer averages eight sequential decode steps in one persistent launch; llama-bench times 32 generated tokens per sample. Results are median per-token times inverted to tokens/s, not a ratio of separate benchmark sessions. llama.cpp uses CUDA, flash attention on, and fp16 K and V. The checkpoint quantizations differ, so the quality comparison below belongs next to the timing comparison. At 8192 on the larger model, both models could not remain resident in VRAM: the harness unloaded and reloaded the megakernel between alternating slices. Loading lies outside both timing windows. Clock snapshots and latency distributions remain in the JSONL records.

Gate v2.1 compares teacher-forced logits with each engine's own Hugging Face fp32 fake-quant reference on the same forced input tokens. It requires a bounded absolute logit difference, treats argmax changes only as permissible near-ties when the reference margin is within the measured error, and repeats a fresh run to check bitwise identity. The 0.6B GPTQ engine passed with maximum absolute logit difference 0.2114, one permitted near-tie flip and no hard flips; repeated logits were bitwise identical. The 1.7B fp32-accumulate engine passed with maximum 0.05154, no hard flips and bitwise repeatability. This is stronger evidence for like-for-like decode arithmetic than comparing two free-running completions: once a near-tie changes one output token, the subsequent inputs differ. It does not imply bitwise equivalence to the reference.

For quality, the llama-perplexity protocol scores WikiText-2 raw test text in 146 non-overlapping 2048-token windows, scoring only the second half of each. GPTQ uses a fake-quant F16 GGUF reconstructed from the packed weights. Q4_0 and Q4_K_M use their own GGUF files, with fp16 KV. These values must not be conflated with the earlier fp32 fake-quant, full-window codec screen: those runs score different token positions and use different arithmetic. The corrected ExLlamaV2 evaluation uses the same tokenization, windows and scored positions, but its own forward arithmetic; its results are identified separately below.

## What failed, and why it matters

We started with A_n simplex weight codes. After group-RMS scaling, the measured weight entropy and kurtosis looked near Gaussian. Under the stated memoryless, conditionally independent weight assumption, the Shannon lower bound for INT4-RTN-g128 distortion was 3.49 bits/weight including scales. Relative to 4.25-bit INT4, no such code could save more than 18% at matched error. Extrapolating measured A4 SQNR suggested about 5% saving, and its quality did not match INT4: A4-k11 at 2.875 bits/weight scored 17.23 PPL versus INT4 at 4.25 bits/weight and 13.43 PPL in the earlier full-window fp32 screen. The A4 matvec path also achieved only 0.74x the INT4 throughput, with shared-memory lookup bank conflicts. The information bound does not rule out a codec that exploits dependence across weights; it killed the proposed memoryless LUT path on these scaled weights. The timeline is `publish/charts/waves.png`.

The first full persistent engine also lost. It achieved 0.96x, 0.80x and 0.66x the separate-kernel CUDA graph at 128, 2048 and 8192. Its 214-register allocation and 256-thread CTAs left eight warps resident per SM for memory-bound phases. A monolithic kernel's register count is the maximum across its tasks, even when most tasks need fewer. The occupancy retry staged activations in shared memory, used 1024-thread CTAs at 64 registers with no spills and reached 32 warps per SM. Its same-session speed relative to the separate graph became 1.42x, 1.50x and 1.44x. A launch-count story alone would have predicted the wrong sign for the first attempt. The working design pays for full-grid barriers; the measured empty-barrier probe was 1.04 microseconds per barrier, with 143 phase barriers per step.

At long context, compressed KV seemed attractive. The tested K8/V4 group-128 cache cut modeled KV reads at context 8192 from 939.524 to 367.002 MB/token, a saving of 572.522 MB/token. Yet the interleaved diagnostic slowed from 4.549 to 6.288 ms/token, or 0.724x throughput. Its full-window codec PPL screen passed at 12.806, but the integrated kernel failed the teacher-forced logit gate at maximum difference 3.2162 against a 0.4900 limit. This kernel is not a deployable alternative, and its failed correctness makes the timing diagnostic rather than a fair speed contender. Packed-cache dequantization and phase work outweighed the ideal byte saving; the experiment did not isolate an exact per-instruction cost. Independently, llama.cpp Q4_K_M with q8_0 KV fell from 184.5 to 164.0 tokens/s at 8192 while PPL stayed near 12.17. Its q4_0 KV option reached PPL 49.3823. Reducing cache bytes by itself was not enough.

A finer synchronization fork lost at context 128: 1.8649 versus 1.7309 ms/token for v2, or 0.9282x throughput. Its extra counters and a two-pass attention path added work; ptxas reported spill stores and loads where v2 had none. The fork changed several ingredients together, so the result cannot assign its entire loss to one. Its first cumulative arrival counter was unsafe: arrivals from a later phase could satisfy an earlier wait. Per-phase counters fixed the race. Static schedule validation could check the intended dependency graph, not whether hand-written counter code faithfully implemented it.

Scaling surfaced a separate failure. The inherited fp16 dot accumulation on Qwen3-1.7B produced maximum finite logit difference 24.6176 and later NaN logits on forced sequences. Keeping packed weights and KV unchanged but accumulating products in fp32 reduced maximum difference to 0.05154, cleared the gate, and still compiled without spills. Only the corrected kernel appears in the performance table. This is a counterexample to assuming that an arithmetic path passing on a smaller model remains safe at the next size.

Finally, ExLlamaV2 0.3.2 did not run Qwen3 correctly out of the box. Its Q/K head normalization followed LayerNorm where Qwen3 requires RMSNorm, and its multi-token Torch SDPA prefill omitted causal masking. On the first test window, the unmodified dense engine scored 59.498 PPL; fixing headnorm alone scored 38.260; disabling that SDPA prefill path as well scored 9.63618 against the HF fp16 reference at 9.63661. Configuration overrides fixed the evaluation without modifying upstream; one-token decode can use SDPA after prefill. The native EXL2 timings above refer to that corrected path, not the stock erroneous one.

## Limits and portability

This is one Turing GPU, two small Qwen3 base models, batch-1 decode, a fixed set of depths, and unlocked clocks. The primary result compares paired engines with different weight quantizers but a measured PPL advantage for GPTQ over Q4_0. It does not dominate Q4_K_M in quality. ExLlamaV2's selected EXL2 head rate differs from the GPTQ format and its host-driven dispatch is part of its measured path. KV compression failed both speed and logit correctness. Nsight Compute counters were unavailable, so occupancy explanations use compilation and controlled ablations, while bandwidth percentages are byte/time proxies. Prefill, multi-request serving, power efficiency, larger models and other GPU generations were not measured.

Ada or Hopper could change the answer in either direction. Different occupancy limits and memory systems would change the cost of making all phases share one register footprint; better kernel dispatch and graph execution could reduce the launch savings; faster attention paths could make fp16 KV less expensive. The codec's assumed information bound is about the tested weight distribution rather than the GPU, but its LUT bottleneck is architecture-sensitive. None of our Turing ratios should be projected onto those devices without rebuilding the baselines and rerunning paired measurements.

## Reproduction and sources

Run `./reproduce.sh all` from the repository root for the packaged gate, schedule validation, paired benchmarks, perplexity measurements and chart generation. Set `LLAMA_CPP_DIR` to a llama.cpp checkout at commit `6011c34ce6099646ccdf0d39a61c6e681477c178` (default `~/llama.cpp`). Standalone chart regeneration is `.venv/bin/python publish/make_charts.py`. GPU jobs go through `scripts/gpu.sh`, with timing jobs taking its `--timing` mode. The script's prerequisites and checkpoint handling are part of the entrypoint; this note does not treat a rerun on a different clock state as an identical latency promise.

- The decode table and `speed_vs_llamacpp.png` come from matched rows in `bench/lm11/results.jsonl` and `bench/lm12/results.jsonl`; timing protocols and rounded reports are in `reports/wave-4/LM-11.md` and `reports/wave-5/LM-12.md`.
- The quality table and `ppl_vs_speed.png` use `bench/lm11/ppl.json`, `bench/lm12/ppl.json`, and the corrected EXL2 measurements in `bench/lm13/ppl_parts.jsonl` and `bench/lm13/results.jsonl`, described in `reports/wave-5/LM-13.md`.
- The byte-derived `roofline.png` uses the `weight_bytes`, `kv_bytes`, `median_ms` and `pct_roofline` fields in those paired JSONL rows, and the read benchmark in `hw/hw.json`. It is not a counter-derived plot.
- The ablation sequence in `waves.png` follows `reports/wave-1.md` through `reports/wave-5.md`, with detailed mechanisms in `reports/wave-3/LM-03b.md`, `reports/wave-4/LM-09.md`, `reports/wave-4/LM-10.md`, and `reports/wave-5/LM-13.md`.
